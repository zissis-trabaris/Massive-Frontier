The Deep Before

The Tide is ancient, patient and purposeful. It has harvested civilisations for longer than the Frontier's stars have burned.

A precursor world under harvest. Reconstruction from artifact imagery.

Long before there were humans to name it, there was the Tide.

It has no homeworld that anyone has found, no language anyone has decoded and no single body that can be killed. It is, in the words of the first survey team to see it and live, a single will wearing ten thousand bodies. It does not build its ships. It grows them from chitin and living resin, and it grows them for one work: the harvest.

The harvest follows a pattern. A civilisation rises. It masters its home, then its sky, then the violent energies that let it cross the dark between stars. Then, sometimes after a century, sometimes after a thousand years, the Tide arrives. Its Harvester swarms strip the worlds of that civilisation to the bedrock. When they leave, nothing living remains, and nothing is wasted.

We know this because we are not the first. Scattered through the Frontier are the remains of those who came before: crystal lattices that were once cities, orbital rings broken like dropped bracelets, vaults of a bone-white material no forge can reproduce. Frontier Command catalogues them as artifacts. Some are older than the Frontier's stars. Every one of them belonged to a civilisation that reached the same threshold humanity has now reached.

The Tide is not a plague and it is not a predator, or not only a predator. Predators hunt when they are hungry. The Tide has never been observed to hurry, to starve, or to take more than it came for. Whatever the harvest is for, it has been going on for a very long time, and it is not finished.

“It is not the harvest that should frighten you. It is that the harvest has a purpose.”

— Deep Space Explorers survey log, author unrecorded
