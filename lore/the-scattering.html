The Scattering

The Tide takes humanity's homeworld. The colony arks flee into the dark with no destination and no way home.

The fall of the Cradle. The arks run for the dark.

Humanity's homeworld has no name in the records of the Frontier. The survivors called it the Cradle, and after a while they stopped speaking of it at all.

The Cradle had reached the threshold. Its first antimatter drives had pushed survey ships past its outer planets; its first colony arks, vast cylinders built to carry a hundred thousand sleepers each, were fitting out in orbit. The Tide found it that same decade.

The records of those days are fragmentary and all agree on one thing: it was fast. The Harvester swarms fell on the Cradle's orbital yards first, then its continents. The defence forces of a dozen nations fought, and were eaten. By the fortieth day the sky was green with bio-light and the arks were launched half-finished, half-crewed and wholly without a destination.

They scattered. That was the only order anyone gave: scatter, so that it cannot take us all at once. Many arks were never heard from again. The survivors spent a century and a half in the dark between stars, a period the ark-born still call the Long Dark. Generations were born, lived and died aboard without seeing a sky.

Two traditions were born in the Long Dark and survive to this day. The pathfinder crews who flew ahead of the arks, charting the black for a world worth landing on, became the Deep Space Explorers. The ark families who came to believe that stopping is what had killed the Cradle, and that a people who never stops can never be harvested, became the Nomad Clans.

“Scatter, so that it cannot take us all at once.”

— Last general order of the Cradle, as recorded in ark logs
